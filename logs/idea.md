Summary of what we decided:

For a hypothetical company that receives a high volume of vendor invoices and payment requests by email, we chose to frame the problem around one specific role, the Accounts Payable (AP) Specialist, rather than a company-wide email gate. We chose this role because “is this email genuine?” depends on who is reading it, and the AP role has a clear decision, concrete evidence (vendor details, bank changes, PO matches, sender domain) and a measurable cost of error (the invoice amount). After discussing what an AP team could want from such a system (binary verdict, risk tiers, ranked queue, fraud-type diagnosis, and so on), we decided the system’s only job is to assess whether an email is genuine or not, with the reasons behind it, while the human always takes the action: pay, do not pay, or verify first. The human keeps the action because money leaves the company on that call, they hold context the system can’t see, and existing controls like approval limits stay intact. We assume, hypothetically, that the AP Specialist confirmed this in a conversation. How the assessment is produced (the Bayesian modelling) is deliberately left for later.
